About

I run quantitative research at MHE Capital, my own systematic futures and crypto operation, and advise private clients on investment and wealth strategy through EM Conseil Invest.

Both rest on the same foundation: nearly seven years in the French Navy (Marine Nationale) as an armament and rescue-equipment technician, ending as deputy manager of the parachute and rescue-equipment workshop aboard the aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle. I was a certifier for return-to-service: the person who signs that a piece of equipment is fit to fly. Nothing leaves the workshop on a feeling.

That is the discipline I bring to markets. I research intraday strategies on CME index futures and crypto venues, from tick-level and full-depth order-book data, under a validation protocol built on pre-registration, out-of-sample splits, multiple-testing correction and surrogate testing. Most of what I test, I refute. The research is largely a record of ideas I killed, and of how I killed them. Execution runs on my own stack: Python, C# and Rust strategies, routed through Rithmic for futures, from execution servers in Chicago and Tokyo.

At EM Conseil Invest the same principle applies to private clients: structure the offer rigorously, state every cost up front, and commit only to what can be evidenced.

Research

Independent research on intraday systematic strategies for CME index futures and crypto venues, run on tick-level and full-depth order-book data under a fixed validation protocol. Most of it is negative: what follows is largely a record of ideas that did not survive testing, and of the tests that killed them. Papers are posted as they are finalised. In the meantime, write to contact.mhecapital@gmail.com for a draft.

A Validation Doctrine for Retail-Scale Systematic Futures Research

Methodology · foundation paper · PDF on request

The protocol every other paper in this series is held to. Pre-registration of the hypothesis before the data is touched, a strict in-sample / out-of-sample split, correction for the number of variants tried, and surrogate testing to establish what a null result looks like on the same series. The standard is deliberately hard to clear: the burden of proof sits with the signal, never with the sceptic.

Order Flow Does Not Predict: A Systematic Refutation of Eight Intraday Signals on NQ Futures

Signal research · refutation · PDF on request

Eight intraday order-flow signals (DeltaSwapRSI, absorption, CVD/price divergence, book imbalance, sweeps and an RSI 65/35 variant among them), tested across two seasons of MNQ and NQ data against surrogate series. None survives. Each either fails to clear transaction costs or is accounted for by drift tails in a rising regime. The paper records what was tested, how, and precisely where each signal breaks.

Two Backtest Bugs That Manufactured Edge

Research engineering · PDF on request

Two defects in my own research harness, each of which produced something that looked like an edge. An NT8 feed handler silently dropped roughly 42% of trades and returned fictitious profit factors; an ORB2 parameter grid returned identical P&L vectors across configurations that were supposed to be distinct. Both are described in full, together with the invariant assertion now permanently built into the harness to catch their recurrence.

Distance-Matched Placebo Testing: Refuting the Gap-Fill Edge

Methodology · refutation · PDF on request

A short methodological note. The apparent gap-fill edge is tested against a placebo built to match the distance distribution of real gaps, the counterfactual a naive test leaves out. Once the comparison is matched, the effect does not survive.

Spread Dynamics on MNQ: Why Pure Spread Capture Is Not Viable at Retail Scale

Microstructure · measurement · PDF on request

A measurement, then a decision. The MNQ spread sits at one tick roughly 42% of the time, with a median of two, against round-turn costs of two ticks. Pure spread capture is arithmetically closed at retail scale. The paper sets out the measurement and the move to a directional grid that followed from it.

How Many Ticks Is a Book-Imbalance Signal Actually Worth on ES and NQ?

Microstructure · execution cost · PDF on request

A book-imbalance signal, I_N, measured on full-depth MBO data for ES and NQ. The signal predicts, and taker execution consumes what it predicts. The paper quantifies, in ticks, the distance between statistically significant and worth trading.

GLFT Market Making: Anchoring the Infinite-Horizon Solution and Why (T−t) Is a Trap

Market making · calibration · PDF on request

From Avellaneda-Stoikov to the Guéant-Lehalle-Fernandez-Tapia infinite-horizon solution, with A and κ calibrated on live flow (γ = 1, A = 760.8, κ = 189.67). Includes an unresolved anomaly, published as it stands rather than smoothed away: the reservation price sits below the mid while inventory is under-weighted.

Latency Economics for a Retail-Scale Operation

Infrastructure · PDF on request

Measured round-trip latencies (3.2 ms to Binance and 3.4 ms to MEXC from Tokyo, against 77 to 88 ms from France, and 0.52 ms to the CME from Chicago), and what each tier permits or forecloses. Infrastructure is treated as a constraint on strategy selection, not as a logistics detail.

Edge Decay: The Overnight Drift Case

Review · life cycle of an edge · PDF on request

The overnight drift as a case study in how long an edge lasts: the phenomenon, its documented dissipation after 2021, and what that implies about the shelf life of any published result. Review and data.

Designing and running sustainable tourism operations, in France and abroad

Master's thesis (VAE), 2025 · ESCAET, MBA Manager of Tourism & Travel Activities (level 7)

The thesis asks how an independent short-let concierge business can be built to be profitable, sustainable and replicable. It draws on the first year of Conciergerie Soléa on the French Riviera, combining market analysis, field observation and internal operating data across the Hyères, Giens and Toulon micro-markets. Its argument is that an independent operator holds its ground against both platform-driven networks and owner self-management only when three things hold together: operations standardised into documented, repeatable procedures; decisions driven by measured data rather than intuition; and a commercial pitch built on evidence, projected net revenue per night and service-level commitments, rather than on promises. The closing section examines the transfer of skills from military aeronautical supply chain to tourism management, and sets out the conditions under which such a model can be duplicated to other territories without diluting it.
